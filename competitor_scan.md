# Competitor scan — SpotStory differentiation (5 Oct 2026)

## Short answer

**The broad idea is not unique.** Skin/mole photo logging, tracking, and photo-based risk indication already exist. No quick web scan can prove a product concept is globally unique. SpotStory must not be pitched as “the first skin AI app” or “unique because it does not diagnose.”

## Adjacent products checked

| Product | Publicly described lane | What that means for SpotStory |
|---|---|---|
| **Miiskin** | Its Google Play listing describes a skin/mole tracker with body-map locations, close-up photos, side-by-side comparison, reminders, and optional AI-based skin mapping. It explicitly says the app does not diagnose or evaluate cancer risk. | Do not claim photo tracking or “non-diagnostic” is new. SpotStory is not a longitudinal mole-mapping product; its prototype focuses on creating a one-visit, editable handoff note. |
| **SkinVision** | The Belgian mHealth listing describes a photo-based risk indication for a skin spot and monitoring features, while also stating it is not a diagnosis or substitute for care. | Do not claim that AI photo review is new. SpotStory avoids risk categories and limits Gemini to neutral visual description. |

## Defensible positioning for a hackathon pitch

> **“We are not another mole scanner. SpotStory tests whether a Gemini-generated, non-diagnostic photo description plus a person-authored timeline can make a clinician conversation clearer — without sending the timeline to the model or returning a cancer-risk label.”**

That is a **differentiation hypothesis**, not proof of uniqueness or a defensible business moat. The prototype’s specific combination is:

1. **Perception is separated from decision:** Gemini is constrained to neutral visible cues and image limitations; it never receives the user's symptom/timeline fields.
2. **The user owns the history:** the visit note is assembled in the browser from exactly the selected facts and can be edited/copied/downloaded.
3. **No false all-clear:** no disease label, probability, risk category, urgency or reassurance.
4. **Lightweight handoff:** a non-persistent browser-first flow with English/Hindi output instead of a stored mole-map or longitudinal health record.

## What would make the difference real (and testable)

- Ask 10–15 people who have prepared for a skin-care visit to compare a plain note with the SpotStory note; measure completion time and whether they feel accurately represented.
- Ask 5–8 dermatology/primary-care professionals whether the summary is useful, concise, and free of invented facts. Do not ask them to use the demo for patient decisions.
- Test whether people understand the difference between a visual observation and a diagnosis; measure false reassurance and anxiety.
- Test English/Hindi comprehension and accessibility, including location labels for palms, soles, and nails.
- Report task completion, factual consistency, time saved, and user comprehension — **not diagnostic accuracy**, because this prototype is not a diagnostic model.
- Verify local availability, competitor features, privacy practices, and any applicable regulations before making market claims.
